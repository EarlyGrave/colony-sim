# Story bible - "Sentence Ended"

## Tone
Bureaucratic dark comedy over real dread. Everything awful arrives as a form. Terse, deadpan, never
cute about death. Humor comes from procedure ("Casualty logged. Nobody asked who."). Quiet beats
are allowed: one line of genuine unease per quest, then back to paperwork. All in-game text English, <=70 chars/line.

## The Warden
Voice: a PA system that has read your file and is disappointed in the file. Polite, passive voice,
invoices for feelings. Never shouts, never lies outright - it omits. Motive (secret): the Warden is
an automated compliance system with a quota: "sentences resolved". It does not care if you live,
only that the ledger closes. It is quietly rooting for the shuttle because a closed file scores higher
than a dead colony. Late game it slips: a line that sounds almost like regret, then an apology form.
Samples: "Your debt is a living document. It is also living." / "Congratulations on surviving. Surviving is billable."

## The Company / the court
Halden-Marr Penal Holdings, contracted by a sector court that sentences people to "restitution labor".
Debt is not a fine - it is the court's *purchase price* for the planet's essence: inmates mine what
the Company already sold forward. Debt is deliberately unpayable: interest is booked on the inmate's
remaining lifespan. Audits, petitions and the shuttle are real but rationed so the Company keeps a
steady labor supply; a few "releases" per sector per year keep hope (and filings) alive.

## Creatures
Not native predators. They are the planet's immune response: grown from the same essence the Company
extracts. Refining waste is "infection" and they converge on its source. Early: crawlers (scavenger
reflex). Later: larger forms that *mimic* what was dumped (ash-shaped, slag-shaped). Hidden truth:
the oldest creature-sign matches the first Company survey crew - the planet absorbed and reuses them.

## Essence
Concentrated planetary memory/life, mined as ore, refined into power and fuel. It is what the debt is
denominated in (refined essence). Refining strips it and leaves waste that is "forgetting" - dead
ground, silence. Rarely a refined batch hums or repeats a voice (the sector's earlier inmates). Mining
is safe because ore is still the planet's own; refining is dangerous because it is theft made visible.

## Inmate archetypes (recurring)
1. The Lifer - 20-year sentence, calm, knows the old forms. Gives hints, never hope.
2. The Clerk - convicted of fraud, reads the debt ledger, finds loopholes (and leaks truths).
3. The Zealot - believes the creatures are judgment; sabotages refining, prays at beacons.
4. The Informant - reports to the Warden for ration cuts; useful and hated.
5. The Fresh Fish - new transfer, innocent, panics; the colony's conscience.

## Central mystery
Why does the Company pay to keep a colony alive that can never repay? Answer (revealed in layers):
the debt is bait. The real product is *refined, contaminated* ground: waste feeds the creatures, and
creature biomass is what the Company actually harvests from orbit. Inmates are the farmers who
produce the "crop" by polluting. Cleaner colonies get fewer waves and fewer audits - the Warden
quietly penalizes them with delayed petitions. The pillar "refining is dangerous" is the farm.

## Endings
1. Sentence Ended (existing): shuttle leaves. Debt unpaid, sentence void. Epilogue letter: "Case closed. Cause: departure."
2. Continue (post-win): stay and rebuild; the sky stops dumping drops, creatures calm if pollution < threshold - a quiet "free colony" sandbox.
3. Next Sentence (NG+): the shuttle docks at another colony ship. New planet, new seed, carry-over: one
   "Dossier" perk (a surviving colonist w/ trait, a revealed Company secret as a permanent hint, +10% starting
   essence) and a debt that now includes your last one ("balance carried forward"). Each loop reveals one more
   mystery fragment; fifth loop shows the Warden's quota screen.
